🐣 How to Take Care of Baby Chickens (Chicks)

Creme Legbar Chickens: From Baby to Adult

Creme Legbars are a beautiful and popular breed known for their sky-blue eggs, friendly personalities, and easygoing nature. They are excellent for backyard flocks. Below is a visual timeline of their growth stages:

Newly hatched Creme Legbar chick. Entering the juvenile stage — feathers start forming. Fully grown adult Creme Legbar hen.

1. Set Up a Brooder

A brooder is a warm, safe enclosure for your chicks to thrive. It mimics the warmth and protection a mother hen provides. Use a large plastic bin, cardboard box, or pet cage lined with pine shavings. Avoid using slippery surfaces like newspaper, which can cause leg problems.

  • Temperature: Begin at 95°F (35°C) during week one.
  • Reduce temperature by 5°F weekly until chicks are fully feathered (around 6 weeks).
  • Use a heat lamp or brooder heater—ensure it’s secure to prevent fires.
  • Keep the brooder draft-free and indoors if possible.

2. Feed and Water

Feeding your chicks the right food and ensuring clean water is critical to their health and development:

  • Provide chick starter feed with 18–20% protein for the first 6–8 weeks.
  • Use shallow, stable containers to prevent drowning and spills.
  • Add pebbles or marbles to waterers during the first week for safety.
  • Change water daily and scrub feeders to prevent bacteria.

3. Keep the Brooder Clean

A clean brooder keeps your chicks healthy and happy. Hygiene plays a big role in preventing illness:

  • Change bedding every 2–3 days or when visibly dirty.
  • Disinfect feeders and waterers regularly.
  • Check daily for "pasty butt" — a common problem where droppings stick to the vent area. Clean gently with warm water if needed.

4. Socialize and Monitor

Baby chicks need attention, gentle handling, and close observation as they grow:

  • Spend time near them so they become accustomed to your voice.
  • Pick them up gently every day to promote trust and reduce fear.
  • Watch for signs of illness or aggressive pecking behavior.
  • Ensure each chick is eating, drinking, and moving around.

5. Prepare for the Coop

When chicks are 6–8 weeks old and have all their feathers, they can move to their outdoor home:

  • Make sure the coop is sturdy, predator-proof, and ventilated.
  • Keep it dry, draft-free, and comfortable with plenty of bedding.
  • Introduce roosts and larger feeders/waterers suitable for pullets.
  • Gradually transition from chick starter to grower feed.

Final tip: Raising baby chickens is rewarding and fun. Give them your time, keep their environment safe and clean, and you'll have a happy, healthy flock in no time! 🐤
